# Typecast business evidence: the problem, in sourced numbers

Compiled November 15, 2026 from public web sources (opened and read unless marked otherwise). Companion to `docs/BUSINESS-CASE.md` and `docs/VIDEO-CLAIMS.md`.

**Rule for every use of this page:** published figures are quoted with their source and scope. Anything Typecast "saves" is a **scenario** built by multiplying a published rate by an assumed number of days or weeks. No customer saving has been measured. Say "could save", "at published rates" and "if", never "saves studios $X".

Each on-location shoot day sits on top of a location that someone had to find, visit and clear first. Typecast addresses the search and shortlist stage, before anyone drives.

## 3. Scenario arithmetic (assumptions shown, nothing measured)

All lines are `published rate x assumed time removed`. The assumed time is the part a pilot has to measure.

| If Typecast removes... | At this published rate | Gross labor value |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 3 billable scout days on a union commercial | $957.58/day | **$2,872.74** |
| 3 scout days on a low-budget shoot | $400 to $600/day | $1,200 to $1,800 |
| 3 scout days on a studio feature | $850 to $1,200/day | $2,550 to $3,600 |
| 1 week of a feature scout's flat deal | $3,000 to $5,500/week | $3,000 to $5,500 |
| 1 week of a union feature location manager | $3,000 to $6,000/week | $3,000 to $6,000 |
| 20 hours of a director's or producer's own searching (no scout on the crew) | $43.44/hour BLS median | $868.80 of time value, not cash |

Limits that must be said out loud or shown on screen:

- A weekly guarantee or salaried scout means released days are **capacity, not cash**. The `/director` calculator already separates these.
- Tool and provider costs are not deducted in this table. The calculator deducts whatever the producer enters.
- The 20-hour line is an opportunity-cost illustration for filmmakers who scout for themselves. The BLS median covers all producers and directors, not indie filmmakers specifically.
- No set-construction, permit or location-rental savings are claimed. See `docs/BUSINESS-CASE.md`.

## 4. Pitch-ready lines

1. "In just three months this year, Greater Los Angeles logged 4,171 on-location shoot days. Every one of them started with someone hunting for a place." (FilmLA Q3 2026 via TheWrap)
2. "Scouts are hired for two weeks to three months per project and can drive 100 to 300 miles a day." (Saturation.io, April 2026)
3. "The 2026 union minimum for a commercial location scout manager is $957.58 a day. If our shortlist removes three billable scout days, that is about $2,873 in gross labor at the published rate, before tool costs. Our pilot has to measure the days." (Local 399 / AICP)
4. "On a low-budget film there is no scout. The director does it. The median producer or director earns $43.44 an hour, so every evening spent scrolling listings is time not spent on the film." (BLS, May 2025)
5. The filmmaker quote, attributed as supplied: "I could save time and money and spend that elsewhere on the film."

## 5. What we still cannot claim

- A measured dollar or hour saving for any production. No pilot has run.
- A market-size number. None was found from a primary source, and none is used.
- A typical hours-per-location or scouting share-of-budget figure. Searches found only blog estimates, so none is quoted.
- That shorter drives are paid time saved. The calculator treats crew travel as a separate opt-in input.
