# AI-dressed sets (World Labs Atlas)

Updated 2026-11-15 02:56 UTC. PRs #41 (walkable sets, whole rooms), #42 (stand-ins, longer walk) and #44 (animated people, card entry) are merged into `dev`; the roller disco hall is in a follow-up PR. Branch `dressed-set`.

Shot ids, Matterport model ids and scan point ids are licensed identifiers and are not written here; they are in the git-ignored `cache/worlds/` records.

## Works (verified)
- `python -m worlds.dress --demo` dressed the top pick's scan point for all 5 demo scenes: images2PosedRGBD (4 real horizon faces, 1280x720, first frame canonical) then atlasChisel (depth-only targets plus a set-dressing prompt). Each step finished in seconds. Estimated camera headings came back within 4° of 0/90/180/270, which independently confirms the viewfinder's face order.
- `GET /api/dressed/{shot_id}` returns the dressed faces with their Atlas camera heading and field of view, the scene, and a fixed AI label. 404 when none exists or when `TYPECAST_ENABLE_DRESSED=0`; 422 on a bad id.
- Viewfinder: an "AI dressing" toggle (D) appears when a dressed set exists. It sweeps a before/after wipe into the 360° view, with a draggable divider ("AI-dressed" | "Real scan") and a persistent "AI-imagined set dressing, not the real room" badge. Outside the dressed frames' vertical coverage the real room shows dimmed. Grabbed frames that include dressing are marked "AI-dressed".
- Walkable set (new): `python -m worlds.dress --splat --draft --scene-id la-empty-shop --allow-spend-credits 500` reconstructed the dressed frames of the LA empty shop as Gaussian splats (posedImages2Splats with `refine: false`). It finished in under 4 minutes and returned one 8.1 MB SPZ (`draftSplat`). `/api/dressed` now returns `splat` (url, mode, scan-point position).
- Viewfinder: when a dressed set has a splat, "Walk the set" (E) appears. The 360° view finishes dressing, then cuts to the splat rendered with three.js + Spark, both vendored and loaded on first use. Same look, lens and grab controls; W A S D moves at eye height. The walk stops at walls and at the edge of what the scan point saw: the limit per direction is read from the splat itself. The badge reads "AI-imagined set dressing, not the real room · draft 3D reconstruction", and grabbed frames stay marked AI-dressed.
- Browser check: `cache/browser-venv/bin/python web/tests/check_walk.py --base-url http://localhost:8006 --shot <shot_id> --executable '/Applications/Google Chrome.app/Contents/MacOS/Google Chrome'` passed in headless Chrome 157 for the whole-room office (walked 3.8 m toward the next scan point, floor plan shown) and the one-point shop (1.5 m to its limit); direct link, 61 fps on an M4 Pro, no browser errors.
- New look on the same geometry: `python -m worlds.dress --redress --scene-id la-office-confrontation --scene "INT. 1980S POLICE PRECINCT BULLPEN - NIGHT. ..."` re-ran atlasChisel on the stored cameras and depth (no re-posing; seconds). The LA office became a night precinct: banker's lamps on the desk rows, a corkboard city map, neon through blinds, filing cabinets. The previous look moved to `dressed/looks/1/`. A draft splat of the new look (7.9 MB, 524,288 splats) finished in about 4 minutes.
- Scale: a one-point capture has no metric scale (0.23 and 0.39 world units per metre in the two sets). The viewer finds the floor in the splat and assumes the scan camera stood 1.5 m above it, so walking speed and wall limits are in metres. Before this fix the empty shop let the camera walk through walls.
- Direct link: `/app/#walk=<shot_id>` (optional `&yaw=<degrees>`) opens the scan point and steps inside, facing the most open direction. Precinct: `http://localhost:8006/app/#walk=<shot_id>`.
- Whole-room set (new, `worlds/room.py`): the LA open-plan office is dressed and reconstructed from 6 real scan points spanning about 11 m, 32 views in all (the limit of one atlasChisel request and of a draft splat). `python -m worlds.room --scene-id la-office-confrontation --points <scan point id prefixes>` pulls the scan points from Matterport, renders overlapping 1280x720 views from their cube faces, estimates depth (images2PosedRGBD), dresses all views in one request with rebuilt depth (`returnDepth`), then `worlds.dress --splat --draft` builds one splat (8.3 MB).
- Refined room (new): the room was re-dressed with a brightly lit precinct scene (lit tubes, brick, corkboards, filing cabinets, desk rows; a few views into the glass rooms stay dark), then `python -m worlds.dress --splat --scene-id la-office-confrontation --allow-spend-credits 5000` ran the refined reconstruction: 2,000,000 splats, 32 MB, 24 minutes. It is sharp and consistent from all six scan points and renders at 61 fps on an M4 Pro. Demo link: `http://localhost:8006/app/#walk=<shot_id>&yaw=0`.
- The refined splat's body-height slice is a clean floor plan (walls, desks, columns). It also showed the doorway between the second and third scan points as a solid partition, which cut the walk in two. The viewer now opens a doorway where the straight line between neighbouring scan points crosses only something thin (up to 0.6 m), because the scan was walked through there.
- Stand-ins (`web/viewfinder-cast.js`): inside a whole-room walk, P brings on five life-size animated people (1.64 to 1.80 m, to scale because the room's scale is known). Two walk the room from scan point to scan point in opposite directions, along a path found on the floor grid, keeping right so they pass each other; three stand off the route. B stands one more where you are looking, facing you. They are six rigged, animated public-domain (CC0) figures by Quaternius, vendored in `web/vendor/people/` with their source pages; if they cannot load, primitive-built mannequins take their place. They are drawn with the splat, hide behind columns and desks, appear on the floor plan and in grabbed frames. Demo link suffix: `&cast=1`.
- The room walk now reaches 6 m from a scan point for a refined splat and requires floor splats under every walkable cell, which keeps it inside the walls. Walkable floor: 76 m², including the aisles round the desk rows.
- Cameras come from the scan, not from Atlas. Atlas's own camera estimate was unreliable here: 2 of 6 scan points were posed inconsistently on both attempts (the ones beside glass partitions), with a layout error of 0.8 m and then 1.9 m RMS against Matterport. Where Atlas was consistent, its turns matched Matterport's scan rotations within 2.4°, and neighbouring distances gave one unit (0.869 world units per metre). So every camera is built from Matterport's measured positions and rotations, scaled by that unit; Atlas supplies depth only. `room/posed.json` and the record's `room.atlas_estimate` keep the comparison.
- Walking a room: `/api/dressed` adds `splat.units_per_m`, `splat.floor_y` and `splat.scan_points`. The viewer builds a 10 cm floor grid from the splat (solid at body height, within 2.2 m of a scan point for a draft, reachable from the start), shows it as a floor plan with the scan points and your position, slides along obstacles, and Shift doubles the pace. The solid-surface threshold scales with splat count.
- Card entry (new): `GET /api/walkable` lists the frames whose scan point has a walkable set (validated by `load_dressed`; empty when `TYPECAST_ENABLE_DRESSED=0`). A card frame on that list shows a filled "Walk the set" button; one press opens the room and steps into the set, facing the middle of the walkable floor, with the people on for a whole-room set. Look around on the same frame still opens the 360° view. In the cached demo runs the precinct scan point is the first frame of a top-three card for the "Glass office confrontation" scene. Verified by `web/tests/check_walk.py` on both sets.
- Roller disco hall (new, draft): the "Roller disco, 1979" scene's top pick, a 40 m barrel-vaulted hall, is a walkable set. Six scan points 6 to 8 m apart, 32 real views; Atlas placed all six within 0.13 m RMS of the scan. `python -m worlds.room --scene-id la-roller-disco --context-faces --brief "..."` sent the four image-model dressed faces (from the 2D dresser) as `contextFrames` and generated the other 28 views depth-guided, so the walk carries the approved look; the record keeps those four faces as `wipe_faces` for the 360° comparison (`load_dressed` prefers them). Then `python -m worlds.dress --splat --draft --scene-id la-roller-disco --allow-spend-credits 500`. Result: the vaulted neon roof, glossy floor and walls hold together; it is a soft draft (half a million splats over a hall ten times the precinct's surface). 288 m² walkable.
- Roller disco hall, refined (new): re-dressed with clear air (the draft's fog blurred it; the floor came out as a polished wooden rink), then `python -m worlds.dress --splat --scene-id la-roller-disco --skip-frames room_05,room_07 --allow-spend-credits 5000`: 26 of the 28 views (two from the first scan point were washed out by glare), 2,000,000 splats, 32.5 MB. Result: sharp vaulted roof with neon, timber walls, rink floor with light dots, a skate rack by the wall; 343 m² walkable with the five people on. The credits were in a second Atlas project, whose key cannot read the first project's assets: `python -m worlds.dress --copy-assets --scene-id la-roller-disco` uploaded the dressed frames again and moved each depth map across (reads with `WORLDLABS_API_KEY_PREVIOUS`, writes with `WORLDLABS_API_KEY`; free).
- The walk never starts hemmed in: when set dressing or stray splats crowd the first 4 m of the line from the start to the next scan point, a path a metre wide is cleared. The precinct is unchanged (76.4 m²).
- Sparse sets: scan points further apart than 4 m take their scale from nearest neighbours; the walk's reach grows to half the widest gap (capped at 6 m); under one floor splat per cell (big hall, glossy floor rebuilt as reflections) the walk is bounded by walls and reach, with solidity read over 3 x 3 cells so haze does not block the floor. The precinct's grid is unchanged (76.4 m²).
- Clean opening (`web/viewfinder-frame.js`): a room now opens without the camera frame, lens bar and shot list, so the whole view, the AI dressing and the walk are seen first. "Frame a shot" (F) brings them on and puts them away; the choice holds for the page. With the frame away, G brings it on instead of grabbing an unseen frame. Verified by `check_walk.py` (hall, precinct) and `check_viewfinder.py --real-index` (9 checks).

## What the runs showed
- A bold scene is what makes this a reveal. The first five dressed sets are plain because their scenes describe the rooms as they already are; the precinct prompt changed the room and reads immediately.
- Standing at the scan point the precinct holds together toward the bullpen (headings 315 to 45). Toward the glass partition one metre away (heading 270) it smears, and above and below about ±30° there is nothing, because the four source frames are 1280x720.
- One metre from the scan point the sets are still coherent but softer. These are drafts from four views that share one optical centre.
- The refined run is what makes the room read as a place: the draft was soft and, in its first dressing, dark; the refined splat of the bright dressing is sharp enough to read the corkboards. Close to a wall (the walk stops 0.25 m away) it still blurs.
- The whole-room draft agrees with itself from all six scan points: the same desks, columns and windows in the same places. This dressing pass came out darker and plainer than the one-point precinct (few props, several near-black views), so it proves the geometry more than the look.

## Not done / limits
- No budget is left for a second refined splat without a top-up.
- The dressed room reads as a dim brick office with corkboards more than as a police station: the restyle follows the real geometry, so the modern desks and mesh chairs stay. Changing the furniture needs the room emptied first (see the defurnishing note in DECISIONS).
- Stand-ins are low-poly figures, not photoreal people, and only stand and walk. One-point sets do not offer them, because their scale is assumed.
- Doorways are inferred from the scan path, not detected: where one is opened, the camera passes through whatever the dressed set drew there.
- 32 views is the ceiling for one consistent dressing request. A larger room needs a second request that uses the first as context; not built.
- The desk area in front of the anchor scan point has no scan point inside it, so it is seen from one side only and is not walkable.
- Matterport's cube up and down faces are not used (their rotation is undocumented), so views tilt at most 5° down and floors right under a scan point come from other scan points.
- One look per scan point is live at a time; the one-point precinct, the dark room draft and earlier looks are archived under `dressed/looks/` (copied, so open viewers keep working). Switching looks inside the walk is not built.
- Walk mode has no touch controls.
- In the 360° comparison the dressed frames are 1280x720, so they cover about ±30° vertically; above and below, the real room shows dimmed.
